## Introduction

Spinal alignment influences how loads are distributed across vertebrae, intervertebral discs, and surrounding tissues. Degeneration changes these structures and can alter alignment in turn. Understanding this interaction is important for studying the development and progression of adult spinal deformity.

Clinical imaging captures different aspects of this process. Radiographs describe alignment under particular positioning and loading conditions, while CT and MRI provide complementary information about bone and soft tissues. Longitudinal studies often contain a mixture of these modalities, making consistent measurement of progression challenging.

This project aims to develop a multimodal approach for measuring spinal alignment and degeneration, and to investigate their relationship with the mechanical environment and reproductive or hormonal status.

## Aim

Develop and evaluate an analysis tool that combines CT, MRI, and radiographs to measure spinal alignment and track structural change over time. The mechanobiology component will examine how altered alignment changes loading and how these changes relate to tissue degeneration and adaptation.

## Task list

- Review methods for measuring spinal alignment, degeneration, and mechanical loading in adult spinal deformity.
- Develop segmentation and anatomical landmark-detection methods across imaging modalities.
- Establish common alignment measures and assess their consistency across modalities and patient positioning.
- Develop a longitudinal workflow for tracking alignment and degeneration in mixed-modality imaging.
- Connect image-derived measurements with computational estimates of the mechanical environment.
- Investigate associations with reproductive or hormonal status using linked clinical information.
- Evaluate measurement reliability and validate automated results against expert assessment.
- Document the workflow and present the findings in a written report and research presentation.

The project can focus on multimodal deep learning, longitudinal measurement, or mechanical modelling. We are exploring Alberta's Tomorrow Project as a potential cohort for investigating reproductive and hormonal factors.

## Expected outcome

A reproducible tool for measuring spinal alignment and degeneration across imaging modalities, together with an evaluation of measurement consistency and longitudinal change. The resulting measurements will support investigation of how mechanical conditions and hormonal factors relate to spinal degeneration.

## MSc and PhD scope

For an **MSc**, the project would focus on a defined component, such as landmark detection, cross-modality alignment measurements, or a longitudinal degeneration analysis, with an assessment of measurement reliability.

For a **PhD**, the work would develop an integrated multimodal framework and use it to investigate how alignment, mechanical loading, and tissue degeneration change together over time, including their relationship with reproductive or hormonal status.

## Student profile

The project is suited to students interested in medical imaging, biomechanics, musculoskeletal health, or machine learning. A background in engineering, computer science, physics, biomedical sciences, or a related field is relevant. Coding experience and an interest in reliable measurement are useful; the methodological focus can be adapted to the student's background.
