## Introduction

Bone metastases alter bone structure and its ability to carry load. Assessing their response to treatment is challenging because changes in lesion size may not capture changes in bone destruction, formation, or mechanical function. Quantitative imaging provides an opportunity to measure these changes and investigate how the local mechanical environment relates to metastatic growth.

This project aims to develop imaging-based measures of bone-metastasis progression and treatment response in patients undergoing immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy. A central question is whether bone structure, image texture, and estimated mechanical loading provide information about lesion behaviour beyond size-based assessment.

## Aim

Develop and evaluate a bone-specific response-assessment approach that combines longitudinal imaging with measures of the local mechanical environment. The mechanobiology component will investigate how changes in load-bearing structure and muscle-generated loading relate to metastatic growth and tissue response.

## Task list

- Review approaches to bone-metastasis response assessment and the role of mechanical conditions in cancer–bone interactions.
- Develop an image-analysis workflow to identify bone and metastatic lesions using deep-learning segmentation.
- Quantify longitudinal changes in lesion morphology, bone structure, and image texture.
- Evaluate image-based finite element models to estimate mechanical conditions around lesions.
- Investigate associations between imaging measures, mechanical conditions, and treatment response.
- Validate the analysis against expert annotations and assess measurement reliability.
- Document the workflow and present the findings in a written report and research presentation.

Depending on the student's interests, the project can focus on segmentation, radiomics, mechanical modelling, or foundation-model-assisted analysis of radiology reports. Report analysis would include validation against clinical annotations. A related research direction with Laurence Lee concerns extending these methods to large-scale breast-cancer cohorts.

## Expected outcome

A reproducible workflow for quantifying bone-metastasis change and an evaluation of how imaging and mechanical measures contribute to response assessment. The work will help identify measures for a bone-specific response framework and generate testable hypotheses about the relationship between loading and metastatic progression.

## MSc and PhD scope

For an **MSc**, the project would focus on one component, such as lesion segmentation, longitudinal radiomics, or local finite element modelling, with a defined validation study.

For a **PhD**, the work would combine method development with longitudinal analysis to investigate cancer–bone mechanobiology and develop a bone-specific response-assessment framework. This could include examining how muscle-generated loading and changes in bone structure relate to metastatic progression.

## Student profile

The project is suited to students in engineering, computer science, physics, biomedical sciences, or related fields who are interested in medical imaging and cancer mechanobiology. Experience with Python, image analysis, machine learning, or finite element modelling is useful. The project can be scoped around one methodological component rather than requiring prior experience in all of them.
